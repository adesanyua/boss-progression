# Gradual dungeon destruction

Opt in per boss using its `dungeon.destruction` object in `config/bossprogression/bosses/*.jsonc` (generated with comments; see `CONFIG_FILES.md`) or a datapack definition:

```json
"destruction": {
  "destroy_after_defeat": true,
  "delay_ticks": 200,
  "blocks_per_tick": 32,
  "structure_block_tag": "bossprogression:structure_cleanup",
  "preserve_bottom_layers": 1,
  "protected_block_tag": "bossprogression:protected_terrain",
  "ruin_chance": 0.35,
  "ruin_max_height": 5
}
```

Defaults: disabled, 200 ticks delay, 32 blocks per tick, 1 preserved bottom layer, protected terrain tag, 0.35 ruin-cluster probability and up to 5 blocks of ruin height above the preserved foundation. Allowed delay 0–72000 ticks; rate 1–128; preserved layers 1–16 (the bottom layer cannot be disabled). Release Evoker, Guardian and Wither definitions explicitly enable this feature. Biome mode ALWAYS ignores it. Legacy procedural arenas are also excluded; use NBT or structure mode.

## How it works

1. When a new encounter site is created, an incremental snapshot is scheduled. Only configured structure-material blocks, or actual NBT template positions, are captured. Boss activation waits for that snapshot to finish.
2. The boss dies: victory and rewards use the normal owner/cycle pipeline. Nearby players (128-block radius) receive a collapse warning. Cleanup does not wait for the owner to be online.
3. After the delay, random centres are selected across the remaining building. Each centre loses a small connected neighbourhood (up to 6 further blocks within a 3×3×3 patch), then the collapse jumps to another location. This creates scattered local cracks, not a top-to-bottom wipe. This is visual disintegration, not a falling-block physics simulation. There are a limited number of break effects. Containers are removed without generating their loot or dropping inventories. Vanilla block drops are not generated by this process.
4. Positions changed by players after encounter registration are protected, including same-material break/replacement. Current state is also compared to the snapshot before each removal: changes from other causes are skipped when their states differ.
5. Snapshot, edit protections, phase, countdown, removal cursor, random-generator state and the current collapse patch are serialized in the per-level encounter attachment. A normal server restart resumes progress. Duplicate death callbacks do not reset or duplicate cleanup.
6. After completion, large lists/sets are discarded. The small encounter reservation remains, so the destroyed site cannot be reused by a new cycle or another player.

The manager uses a shared 1024-work-unit budget per level and a soft 2 ms time slice, with round-robin fairness. Capture work is capped at 256 positions per job per tick. Removal is capped by `blocks_per_tick`. A synchronous chunk load can exceed the time slice; this is not a hard wall-clock guarantee. Work resumes without requiring an online owner or loaded player chunk, so chunks may be loaded by cleanup.

Limits: 2 million candidate positions (including overlapping piece scans) and 262144 captured blocks per encounter. Hitting a limit disables cleanup with a warning rather than partially guessing the rest. Edit protection is also bounded; overflow aborts cleanup conservatively. Preparing a large snapshot can take time; the invitation may already exist while activation is waiting.

## NBT mode

Candidates come from the template's `blocks` list, not from every position in its enclosing box. Air, structure void/block and bedrock are never removed. The original terrain overwritten when the template was placed is NOT restored. Blocks subsequently modified by players are left behind deliberately. NBT templates are removed except for small grounded ruin fragments, foundation layers, protected terrain materials and protected/changed blocks. Stone and other terrain materials inside a template remain by default as well; use a custom protected terrain tag if you deliberately want stone walls removed. Protection takes priority over the structure-material allow-list.

Saved entities, dropped items and other mobs are not cleanup targets. This feature removes blocks, not all entities in the building.

## Foundation and ground protection

For both NBT and structure mode, `preserve_bottom_layers` keeps the lowest Y layer(s) of the overall saved dungeon bounds. For NBT this is relative to the template's minimum corner; if the template has empty layers below its real floor, increase this count accordingly. For world structures it uses the lowest Y across all piece boxes, not a separate protected floor for every upper-floor piece.

The separate `protected_block_tag` veto applies at every height, both during capture and immediately before deletion, including older persisted queues. The default `bossprogression:protected_terrain` protects dirt/grass, sand, gravel, clay/mud, natural stone types, Nether terrain, end stone, water and lava. Thus ground is not accidentally removed just because a broad custom structure tag matches it. Foundations and soil are intentionally left as ruins; this is not restoration of the original overwritten terrain.

## Grounded ruins

Cleanup deliberately leaves small irregular sections of the lower walls, instead of erasing every wall down to the foundation. Nearby columns share organically shaped clusters (jittered spatial cells), while fragment height tapers toward cluster edges and varies from column to column. This is not a random sprinkling of isolated blocks.

- `ruin_chance`: finite 0–1, default 0.35; probability of keeping a spatial cluster, **not an exact fraction of all building blocks**. 0 restores full removal of eligible blocks above the protected foundation; 1 keeps every eligible low-level cluster.
- `ruin_max_height`: 1–12, default 5, measured above the top preserved foundation layer. Most fragments are shorter than the cap. Roofs and high floors continue to collapse.
- Only full-collision structural blocks without block entities qualify. Every retained column must have uninterrupted solid support down to the preserved foundation plane. Containers and unsupported roof/floor fragments are still removed; this does not spawn new rubble or falling-block entities.
- The mask is deterministic from saved region bounds and world coordinates, independent of the removal RNG/order. Restarting a pending job does not reroll surviving fragments. The settings are saved with each job; older jobs missing these two fields inherit the new defaults.
- Ground/foundation protections still take precedence. Existing protected materials or player edits can leave additional remnants outside the ruin mask. Unusual templates with empty foundation layers may leave fewer wall fragments; ruins are relative to the saved bounds, not a physical ground reconstruction.

## Existing structures

Minecraft does not keep an exact per-block ownership map for generated structures. We therefore capture only matching materials inside individual `StructurePiece` boxes. The default material tag supports common mansion, monument and fortress building/decorative materials. It excludes ordinary dirt, natural stone, sand, water and lava rather than clearing an entire volume.

This is deliberately conservative: some blocks can remain (e.g. soil under gardens), so full erasure of every possible vanilla/modded structure is NOT guaranteed. Player structures present before registration, or natural blocks made of an allowed material inside the piece boxes, cannot be distinguished from the dungeon and may be removed. Back up the world and do not claim structures already inhabited by players.

For modded dungeons, provide an appropriate custom **block tag** and reference it in `structure_block_tag`. Example datapack file `data/mypack/tags/block/my_dungeon_materials.json`:

```json
{
  "replace": false,
  "values": ["minecraft:stone_bricks", "minecraft:chest", "othermod:dungeon_bricks"]
}
```

Do not weaken the protected terrain tag unless you intentionally accept that risk. A tag added after the snapshot is complete does not change the already captured block list. Reload/config edits do not retrofit cleanup onto old saved encounters.

## Safety / operational behavior

- Rewards and offline victory reconciliation are independent of destruction; reset does not cancel a running queue.
- Exiting the structure before collapse is the player's responsibility. No automatic evacuation or party handling is added.
- Ruined structures retain vanilla worldgen metadata: `/locate structure` can still point to the ruins. The Boss Progression reservation prevents reactivation.
- Old encounters lacking a snapshot are NOT retrospectively destroyed.
- Standard restart persistence is covered; world/block/attachment saves are not a single crash-atomic transaction.
- Third-party block callbacks, piston edits that restore an identical state, and native scheduled physics may have behavior outside the vanilla no-drop/player-edit checks. No claim of compatibility with every modded block is made.

## Verification

`./gradlew runGameTestServer --no-configuration-cache` runs `DungeonDestructionTests` in isolated test structures. The Gradle run now uses `run/gametest/` (separate from the ordinary dedicated server's `run/world` and single-player saves):

- `nbtResumeNoDrops`: bounded gradual removal, protected same-material replacement survives NBT round-trip, removal cursor resumes, outside block survives, chest inventory produces no item entities, finished cleanup is idempotent.
- `structureKeepsTerrainAndEdits`: tagged building block disappears, dirt remains, changed block state remains.
- `biomeAndDisabledAreIgnored`: biome mode and default-off settings produce no cleanup job.
- `chaoticPatchesPreserveFoundation`: removal reaches a lower wall before completing the top layer, survives a queue round-trip, removes all unprotected walls without omissions, and preserves the bottom layer and dirt above it.

- `groundedRuinsSurviveRestart`: a wall/roof fixture retains small grounded wall remnants after a mid-collapse round-trip; upper roof and unsupported fragments disappear, foundation stays, and containers produce no drops.

All 5 destruction tests (12 required GameTests total including mob settings and config/sync) passed during implementation. Build and ordinary dedicated startup are also checked.

Manual checks still required: visual appearance/amount of ruins in actual houses, full mansion/monument/fortress collapse and material coverage, custom NBT placement and capture, multi-player block edits, logout/restart mid-countdown/removal, many concurrent large encounters, fluid/door/physics behavior, modded structure materials, and all reward/reset edge cases while the queue is running.
